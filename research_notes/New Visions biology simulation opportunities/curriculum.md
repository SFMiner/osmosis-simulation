# New Visions biology curriculum identity and unit map

## Which curriculum matches the user's Marathon Runner example?

### Takeaway
The match is New Visions for Public Schools' current six-unit Biology course, explicitly designated high-school NGSS/NYSSLS. Treat middle school as Sean's teaching audience, not as the publisher's grade-band label.

### Cited Findings
- The official course page labels it full-course high-school biology, aligned to NGSS and NYSSLS. Its six units, in order, are Marathon Runner; Humans vs. Bacteria; Evolution of Sick Humans; Saving the Mountain Lion; Food for All; Woolly Mammoth. — [Course sequence](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/biology-curricular-materials)
- The publisher now recommends Biology and Earth & Space Science, with legacy Living Environment and Earth Science materials available by contacting its science team. These should not be conflated with the current Biology sequence. — [Official FAQ](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum-instruction-faq)
- The public pages state that users register for free materials, with registration intended for educators and family members. Many teacher/student material and slide links resolved only to the login trigger. Course-map Google Doc retrieval redirected to sign-in. — [Getting started](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/getting-started)
- New Visions-created materials use CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; partner materials can have different licenses. — [Course sequence](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/biology-curricular-materials)

### Inferences
- The user's named unit establishes course identity without needing to ask them to clarify before doing useful research.
- Build concepts accessible to middle-school learners while retaining this course's phenomena and investigation questions; do not assert an official middle-school edition.

### Gaps
- No separate official middle-school Biology curriculum was found in this search.
- Exact latest logged-in revisions cannot be verified. Public indexed SY25-26 PDFs are useful but should be labeled by edition; do not call them definitively the current SY26-27 files.

## What are the current unit storylines and simulation entry points?

### Takeaway
The public current pages provide specific phenomena and storyline names for all six units. These are sufficient to align proposed simulations, while detailed activity duplication needs teacher-material inspection.

### Cited Findings
- **U1 Marathon Runner:** homeostasis and feedback; woman becomes disoriented then comatose after a marathon. Storyline sections: Gas Exchange and Cellular Respiration; Muscles & Energy; Human Thermoregulation; Water Balance. Students develop an explanation using interacting body systems. Listed length 6–7 weeks. — [U1](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology/marathon-runner)
- **U2 Humans vs. Bacteria:** natural selection and evolution. Sections: The Black Death; Antibiotic Resistance; The Microbiome; Cooperation and Survival. Ends with evaluating how to prevent a future cholera outbreak. Listed length 6–7 weeks. — [U2](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology/humans-vs-bacteria)
- **U3 Evolution of Sick Humans:** investigates protein-level mechanisms and mismatch between bodies and modern environments. Sections: Lactase Persistence; Leptin Resistance; Circadian Rhythms; Common Ancestry. Culminates in designing a healthy school. Listed length 6–7 weeks. — [U3](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology/evolution-of-sick-humans)
- **U4 Saving the Mountain Lion:** genetics and variation. Connecticut road-killed lion's origins investigated using microsatellite data. Sections: Mountain Lion Populations; Sexual Reproduction (Optional); Genetic Variation; Engineering Gene Flow. Conservation solutions address isolated populations and low genetic diversity. Listed length 6–7 weeks. — [U4](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology/saving-the-mountain-lion)
- **U5 Food for All:** matter and energy through the historical pellagra epidemic, corn dependence, food processing, niacin availability, and systemic inequities. Sections: Neolithic Revolution; The SuperFood That Changed The World; Infectious Agent or Insufficient Diet; Food For Plants. Listed length 6–7 weeks. Note: current overview is more specific than a generic food-security unit. — [U5](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology/food-for-all)
- **U6 Woolly Mammoth:** ecology and human impacts, extinction and whether de-extinction is a useful response to biodiversity loss. Sections: Tuskless Elephants; Coral Bleaching; Kelp Forest; Passenger Pigeon. Students already use simulations. Listed length 6–7 weeks. — [U6](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology/woolly-mammoth)
- U6 includes HS-LS4-6, requiring creation or revision of a simulation to test an intervention's effects on biodiversity. U5 includes computational representations of carrying capacity. — [Course sequence](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/biology-curricular-materials)

### Inferences
- Strong build candidates by storyline: U1 thermoregulation or coupled kidney/water balance; U2 population-level antibiotic selection; U3 lactase action and gene expression; U4 wildlife corridors and gene flow; U5 photosynthesis/respiration and limiting resources; U6 kelp-urchin-otter disturbance and recovery.
- U4 corridor placement may be unusually useful because students can test conservation designs over generations, a timescale inaccessible to classroom observation.
- U6 requires an audit of existing simulations before rebuilding; the official overview explicitly already includes them.

### Gaps
- These candidates are design judgments, not a publisher recommendation or proof that the curriculum lacks similar activities.
- No verified activity-level detail from U2–U6 PDFs in this scoped research; other agents are investigating that.

## How does the existing osmosis simulator connect to the onion lab?

### Takeaway
The exact onion-cell investigation is verified in the official SY25-26 student materials, followed immediately by a kidney investigation. A bridge from cell-level water movement to whole-body feedback is the clearest adjacent opportunity.

### Cited Findings
- SY25-26 U1 student materials contain the onion-cell investigation on printed pages 132–134. Students observe onion cells, expose them to salt solution, then distilled water, and convert drawings into models showing concentration and net water movement. Hypotonic, hypertonic, and isotonic conditions are compared. — [U1 student PDF](https://newvisions-uploads-production.s3.amazonaws.com/bio_U1/BIO%20%7C%20U1%20%7C%20Marathon%20Runner%20%7C%20Student%20Materials%20%7C%20SY%2025-26.pdf)
- Printed pages 135–138 then use kidney input/output data under no, medium, and high water intake during exercise. Students compare blood and urine solutes, model kidney behavior, and predict effects of sports drinks. The later reading connects thirst and hormone-mediated water reabsorption to regulation. — [U1 student PDF](https://newvisions-uploads-production.s3.amazonaws.com/bio_U1/BIO%20%7C%20U1%20%7C%20Marathon%20Runner%20%7C%20Student%20Materials%20%7C%20SY%2025-26.pdf)
- The official teacher-material search index identifies both Osmosis in Onion Cells Investigation and Osmoregulation in the Kidney Investigation under Water Balance 5E. The teacher PDF itself failed full retrieval; the student PDF was successfully opened. — [U1 teacher PDF, indexed evidence](https://newvisions-uploads-production.s3.amazonaws.com/bio_U1/BIO%20%7C%20U1%20%7C%20Marathon%20Runner%20%7C%20Teacher%20Materials%20%7C%20SY%2025-26.pdf)

### Inferences
- Sean's simulator already fills a real microscopy-to-mechanism gap; a second simulator could link drinking, sweating, blood concentration, kidney reabsorption, urine volume, and cell volume over time.
- Retain separate plant-cell and animal-cell behavior if showing an onion-to-human comparison; visual transfer should not imply identical structural responses.

### Gaps
- I did not inspect the local simulator; its implemented features are known only from user context, so no claim is made about missing features.
