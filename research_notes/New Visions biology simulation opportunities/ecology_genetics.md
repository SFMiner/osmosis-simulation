# Ecology, genetics, and reproduction simulation opportunities

## Where are the verified curriculum placements?

### Takeaway
The curriculum containing Marathon Runner is officially high-school Biology; these are proposed adaptations for middle-school learners, not an official middle-school scope. The best distinctive opportunities are a plant light/dark gas-exchange model, a mountain-lion gene-flow model, and a kelp-forest disturbance model.

### Cited Findings
- New Visions identifies the course as full-course high-school biology designed for NGSS/NYSSLS. [Course](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology)
- Unit 4, Saving the Mountain Lion, contains Mountain Lion Populations, optional Sexual Reproduction, Genetic Variation, and Engineering Gene Flow. Its conservation problem concerns isolated populations and variation. [Unit 4](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology/saving-the-mountain-lion)
- Unit 5, Food for All, places Neolithic Revolution before The SuperFood That Changed the World, Infectious Agent or Insufficient Diet, and Food for Plants. The anchor is pellagra and inequitable access to nutritious food. [Unit 5](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology/food-for-all)
- Unit 6, Woolly Mammoth, includes Tuskless Elephants, Coral Bleaching, Kelp Forest, and Passenger Pigeon. It culminates in evaluating extinction explanations and de-extinction as a biodiversity intervention. [Unit 6](https://www.newvisions.org/curriculum/science/biology/woolly-mammoth)
- Unit 4's Engineering Gene Flow investigation uses colored marbles, paper bags, and a genetic-diversity calculator; students examine introducing Texas individuals into Florida. The teacher guide explicitly distinguishes middle-school allele inheritance from high-school work on meiosis, drift, and gene flow. - Unit 5 teacher materials use light/dark plant investigations with CO2/O2 observations, and a decomposition investigation built around food time-lapse videos. - Kelp & Barrens is a data-and-model investigation involving maps, disturbance histories, ecosystem cards, and stable ecosystem states. [Unit 6 teacher materials, SY22-23, pp.94-100](https://newvisions-uploads-production.s3.amazonaws.com/bio_U6/BIO%20%7C%20U6%20%7C%20Woolly%20Mammoth%20%7C%20Teacher%20Materials%20%7C%20SY%2022-23.pdf)

### Gaps
Current lesson downloads generally lead to registration. Publicly indexed primary teacher PDFs vary by year (U4 24-25; U5/U6 22-23), so lesson-level details need comparison with Sean's current edition before implementation. A 25-26 U5 student document still includes the mouse simulation.

## Which focused simulations would be useful?

### Takeaway
Prefer one causal mechanism and one comparison per tool. Expose invisible processes or let students repeat generations/time that a physical lesson cannot easily show.

### Cited Findings
The placements below derive from the official unit pages and teacher guides linked above; controls, MVP scope, misconceptions, and ranking are proposed instructional designs.

### Inferences
1. **Plant in light versus darkness — strongest direct lab companion.** Placement: U5, The SuperFood That Changed the World, beside the gas-exchange investigation. Controls: light/dark, number of plant sprigs, starting CO2; observations: paired containers, CO2/O2 graphs, sugar store, arrows for photosynthesis and respiration shown separately. Target misconception: plants only respire at night, or obtain their carbon mass from soil. MVP: two identical closed containers, light toggles, reset and save comparison. For middle school use molecule icons and qualitative low/medium/high amounts; omit biochemical pathways. Ensure respiration remains active in light and show net gas change as the difference between two processes. This model is especially analogous to Sean's onion-cell supplement.
2. **Mountain-lion genetic rescue — strongest distinctive conservation build.** Placement: U4 Engineering Gene Flow, after the marble investigation. Controls: isolated population size, number and allele types of immigrant lions, open/closed corridor. Observations: offspring allele pairs and population variation across generations, replicated outcomes. Target misconception: simply adding more identical individuals creates genetic diversity; individual lions consciously seek genes. MVP: two habitat patches, one autosomal marker represented by colored tokens, random inheritance, five generations, compare isolated versus connected. Middle-school mode emphasizes one allele from each parent and counts of variants; heterozygosity formulas optional. Distinguish redistribution of existing alleles from mutation creating new alleles. Conservation effects should be qualitative unless independently calibrated; avoid predicting real survival from one toy marker.
3. **Kelp forest disturbance and recovery — strongest ecosystem build.** Placement: U6 Kelp Forest, after Kelp & Barrens data analysis and before revising ecosystem models. Controls: predator loss, urchin removal, disturbance duration; observe kelp cover, urchins, predators, recovery time. Target misconception: removing any organism has only a direct effect, or ending a disturbance guarantees instant recovery. MVP: kelp/urchin/predator system, two side-by-side runs, one disturbance and one restoration action. Middle-school default uses population graphs and causal arrows; optional advanced mode explores different recovery thresholds. Treat model results as conditional on assumptions, not proof of what caused a particular historical site to collapse. Do not imply every kelp system has identical predators or dynamics.
4. **Where does decomposing food go?** Placement: U5 Infectious Agent or Insufficient Diet, following decomposition videos. Controls: moisture, temperature, open versus sealed container; observe food carbon moving into decomposers and CO2, water loss, total mass inside the chosen system boundary. Target misconception: disappearance means matter was destroyed; visible mold directly measures nutritional value. MVP: one generic food, decomposer population, carbon token accounting, a boundary toggle, no food-brand comparison. Good middle-school fit for conservation of matter. Do not hard-code processed food as non-decomposing or infer nutrition from decomposition rate; that would turn a useful model into an unsupported rule. Model energy separately from matter.
5. **Coral heat pulse and recovery — lower priority because good resources exist.** Placement: U6 Coral Bleaching after real heat-stress data and existing HHMI animation. Controls: temperature anomaly and duration, then return to baseline; observations: symbiont abundance, coral energy reserve, bleaching/recovery state. Target misconception: white coral is already dead or heating has immediate all-or-nothing effects. MVP: two colonies exposed to different pulse lengths, one graph, clear model assumptions. Middle-school fit is visual symbiosis; avoid molecule-level oxidative chemistry. A new tool earns its place only through repeated controlled comparisons and time-course data absent from a simple animation; first inspect the existing NASA and HHMI options.

### Gaps
These are specifications, not scientifically calibrated simulations. Parameters and edge cases would require biological review during building. No claim is made that there are no comparable commercial or public tools anywhere.

## What already exists, and what should not be duplicated?

### Takeaway
Do not start with generic carrying capacity or an animation of coral bleaching. New Visions already assigns the former, and HHMI/NASA offer strong starting points for the latter. Repeated runs, saved comparisons, and precise alignment with the physical lesson are the potential added value.

### Cited Findings
- U5's Neolithic Revolution already assigns Jon Darkow's Population Dynamics of White Footed Mouse simulation. The SY25-26 student materials ask partners to select two environmental variables and record effects. [Current student materials p.20](https://newvisions-uploads-production.s3.amazonaws.com/bio_U5/BIO%20%7C%20U5%20%7C%20Food%20for%20All%20%7C%20Student%20Materials%20%7C%20SY%2025-26.pdf) | [Assigned model](https://exchange.iseesystems.com/public/jondarkow/population-dynamics-of-white-footed-mouse/index.html#page2)
- U6 lists EcoOcean overfishing in Tuskless Elephants and Annenberg's Carbon Cycle Lab in Coral Bleaching. Thus the elephant sequence already uses simulation, although its assigned tool is about fish harvest rather than elephant inheritance. [U6 teacher materials p.20](https://newvisions-uploads-production.s3.amazonaws.com/bio_U6/BIO%20%7C%20U6%20%7C%20Woolly%20Mammoth%20%7C%20Teacher%20Materials%20%7C%20SY%2022-23.pdf) | [EcoOcean](https://www.ecoocean.de/play-online/)
- HHMI has a coral-bleaching animation illustrating symbionts and temperature stress. [HHMI animation](https://www.biointeractive.org/classroom-resources/coral-bleaching)
- HHMI's interactive exploration additionally includes pause points, questions, text, illustrations, and widgets covering symbiosis, cells, reproduction, and photosynthesis. [HHMI educator guide](https://www.biointeractive.org/sites/default/files/media/file/2023-03/InteractiveCoralBleaching-Educator-ivideo.pdf)
- NASA lists a coral game for grades K-4 and 5-8 in which students change temperature and pollution. [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/stem-content/coral-bleaching/)

### Inferences
Prioritize plant gas exchange, gene flow, and kelp recovery. Keep the physical lab/data investigations and add short predict-test-explain comparisons. The gene-flow tool should complement the existing marbles/calculator; its added value is multiple generations and habitat connectivity.

### Gaps
Existing simulations were identified from authoritative descriptions and curriculum assignments, not interactively usability-tested. Do not claim their links are broken or their UX is poor. Public access to a page does not establish current classroom device compatibility.
